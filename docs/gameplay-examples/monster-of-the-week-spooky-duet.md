# Monster of the Week — Spooky Duet Example

## Purpose
A **fiction-first duet example** showing how *Monster of the Week* flows through conversation until the fiction triggers a Move, with clear **2d6 success bands** and player-facing narrative choices.

## Cast
- **Keeper (GM)**
- **Player (Sam)** playing **Chloe**, a teenager using **The Spooky** playbook

## Setting
An abandoned, fog-choked carnival at **midnight**. The target is a creature stealing memories.

---

## Scene Start

**Keeper:** You step through the rusted turnstiles of the carnival. The fog is incredibly thick, clinging to the rotting wood of the boardwalk. It's dead silent, except for the faint, tinny sound of calliope music coming from the Funhouse at the end of the pier. You know the Memory-Thief is in there. What do you do?

**Player (Sam):** I'm not walking into a Funhouse blind. I want to close my eyes, reach out with my Spooky powers, and try to sense the creature's psychic footprint. I'm looking for cold spots or the feeling of static electricity that usually follows it.

**Keeper:** Awesome. Because you are actively using your dark powers to sense the supernatural, that triggers the **Use Magic** move. Roll `2d6` and add your **Weird** stat.

**Player (Sam):** My Weird is `+2`. Let's see...

---

## Mechanical Break 1 — Use Magic

**Roll:** `2d6 = 3 and 2`

**Modifier:** `+2 (Weird)`

**Total:** `7`

- `10+` = full success
- `7–9` = mixed success
- `6-` = Keeper makes a hard move

**Keeper:** A 7 is a **Mixed Success**. You get what you want, but there's a narrative cost or a glitch. The magic works, but it draws unwanted attention, takes a toll on you, or goes slightly awry. Which sounds worst to you right now?

**Player (Sam):** Oh, definitely **drawing unwanted attention**. Chloe isn't great at being subtle.

**Keeper:** Perfect. You close your eyes, and the air temperature plummets. You feel that static charge, and you can pinpoint exactly where the creature is: it's **not** in the Funhouse. It's directly beneath you, clinging to the wooden pilings under the boardwalk.

But because of the glitch, your magical pulse acted like a flare in the dark. The tinny calliope music abruptly stops. The wooden planks beneath your boots start to groan and splinter as something massive starts violently digging its way up toward you. What do you do?

**Player (Sam):** I scramble backward. I want to pull the heavy iron crowbar out of my backpack and brace myself. If it breaks through the wood, I'm swinging for its head.

**Keeper:** The planks explode upward in a shower of rotten wood and sea salt. A creature that looks like a tangled mass of wet theater masks and elongated limbs hauls itself onto the pier. It lunges at you, multiple arms reaching for your face to drain you.

You're swinging the crowbar to fight back, which triggers **Kick Some Ass**. Roll `2d6` plus your **Tough** stat.

**Player (Sam):** My Tough is only `+1`. I'm a Spooky, not a fighter.

---

## Mechanical Break 2 — Kick Some Ass

**Roll:** `2d6 = 5 and 4`

**Modifier:** `+1 (Tough)`

**Total:** `10`

**Keeper:** A 10 is a **Strong Hit**. You inflict your weapon's harm, and because you rolled `10+`, you get to choose one extra effect from the *Kick Some Ass* list. You can take less harm, force the monster where you want it, or inflict terrible harm (`+1 damage`).

**Player (Sam):** I want to **force it where I want it**. I want to hit it so hard that I knock it backward, right off the edge of the pier and into the freezing water below.

**Keeper:** Done. Your crowbar connects with a sickening **CRACK** against one of its porcelain mask-faces. It shrieks—a sound like a hundred overlapping whispers—and loses its footing. It tumbles backward over the rusted railing and splashes into the black, freezing ocean.

You take `1 Harm` from its glancing claws before it falls, but you've bought yourself some time. You hear it thrashing in the water below.

---

## Why this example is useful in MONITOR

- **Conversation-first resolution** with no initiative or turn grid
- **Moves triggered by fiction**, not by menu selection
- **Mixed success escalation** that keeps momentum high
- **Player choice inside mechanics** on a strong hit
- **A strong benchmark for horror-forward, improvisational duet play**
