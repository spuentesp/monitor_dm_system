## 2. Real-GM Craft Principles (Reference Frame)

These patterns emerge from studying Death in Space, Vampire: The Masquerade, and
classic GM methodology (Dungeon World, PBTA, OSR referee advice sections):

### 2.1 Play to find out

A good GM does not know what will happen. They set up a situation and react.
MONITOR should start with minimal setup and let the player's first words define
the scene — not pre-generate a backstory form before they've said anything.

**VtM20 principle:** "Your job is not to tell a story. Your job is to help create one."
**DiS principle:** "The referee answers questions about the world; players answer questions about themselves."

### 2.2 One question at a time

Real GMs ask one thing and truly listen. Multiple-part questions fragment attention
and produce exhausted, shallow answers. The current triple-message opening violates
this every single session.

### 2.3 Agency over assignment

Dice rolls in TTRPG are optional agreements between GM and player.
When a GM rolls for the player without asking — especially at the very start of
the session before the player exists in fiction — it breaks agency.
An empty character sheet is a creative invitation, not a deficit.

**Correct pattern (DiS, VtM20):**
1. GM describes the situation
2. Player describes who they are
3. Player *chooses* whether to roll (or the system rolls *for* a specific declared action)
4. Rolls reflect fiction, not precede it

### 2.4 Failure is interesting, not an error

When MONITOR hits an LLM or pipeline failure, it currently surfaces a technical
string. Real GMs handle stalling gracefully — they deflect, describe ambient
detail, or ask the player a question. The error path is a GM moment.

### 2.5 The GM speaks last (in exposition)

A real session opening is: the GM paints a brief scene, then hands it to the
player with one question. All the system-level context (what city, what year,
what game) is absorbed into the fiction, not announced as metadata.
