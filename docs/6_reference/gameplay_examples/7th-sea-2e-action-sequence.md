# 7th Sea 2e — Action Sequence Example

## Purpose
A **cinematic swashbuckling example** showing declared risks, Raises, and success-with-style rather than binary pass/fail.

## Cast
- **Game Master (GM)**
- **Player (Elena)** playing **Donatello**, a Vodacce duelist and courtier

## Setting
A grand masquerade ball in a Vodacce palazzo at **midnight**.

---

## Scene Start

**GM:** The clock chimes midnight. Just as the masks are supposed to come off, the glass skylight shatters! Three assassins in black silk drop into the ballroom. One of them grabs the visiting Castillian ambassador and holds a stiletto to his throat. The crowd erupts into panic. We are entering an Action Sequence.

**Player (Elena):** Donatello isn't going to let an ambassador die at his host's party—it's incredibly bad manners. I draw my rapier and want to vault over the banquet table to intercept the assassin before he can slit the ambassador's throat.

**GM:** Excellent. Your Approach is leaping over the table to engage the assassin. You'll be rolling **Finesse + Athletics**.

Before you roll, here is the setup for this round:

- **Consequence 1:** The panicked nobles are stampeding toward the doors. An elderly duchess is about to be trampled. *(Costs 1 Raise to prevent.)*
- **Consequence 2:** The assassin holding the ambassador throws a secondary dagger right at your chest. *(Costs 1 Raise to avoid taking 1 Wound.)*
- **Opportunity:** One of the other assassins drops a sealed wax letter from their cloak as they land. *(Costs 1 Raise to snatch it up as you run by.)*

**Player (Elena):** Okay, so to do everything—stop the assassin, save the duchess, dodge the dagger, and grab the letter—I need 4 Raises. My Finesse is 3, and my Athletics is 3. That gives me 6 dice. I'm also going to spend a Hero Point to add 3 bonus dice.

**GM:** I love it. Roll your 9 dice and let's see how many sets of 10 (Raises) you get.

---

## Mechanical Break

**Roll:** `10, 9, 8, 7, 5, 4, 3, 2, 2`

**Sets of 10 / Raises:**
- `10` = 1 Raise
- `9 + 2` = 1 Raise
- `8 + 3` = 1 Raise
- `7 + 4` = 1 Raise

**Result:** **4 Raises**

---

## Outcome

**GM:** Four Raises! You have exactly enough to do everything perfectly. Tell me how Donatello pulls this off.

**Player (Elena):** It's pure poetry in motion.

- **First Raise:** I sprint toward the banquet table, but as I see the stampeding crowd, I kick a heavy oak chair directly into the path of the mob, creating a wedge that diverts them around the fallen duchess.
- **Second Raise:** I use the momentum of my sprint to dive into a slide across the polished marble floor. The dagger sails right over my face, slicing off a few strands of my hair.
- **Third Raise:** I scoop up the letter with my left hand mid-slide, tucking it into my doublet.
- **Fourth Raise:** I spring up from the slide right in front of the lead assassin, parrying his stiletto away from the ambassador's throat with my rapier in a flurry of sparks. "Pardon the intrusion," Donatello says with a wink. "But I believe this dance is mine."

**GM:** The ambassador gasps and stumbles backward, completely safe. The assassin glares at you, realizing he's lost his hostage. The ballroom is in chaos, but the target is secure. Now it's the assassin's turn, and he's going to spend a Raise to strike back...

---

## What this example is useful for in MONITOR

- **Risk declaration before the roll**
- **Success budget thinking** rather than yes/no resolution
- **Multiple simultaneous stakes** in a single action beat
- **Player-visible consequences and opportunities** before commitment
- **Stylized narration** that matches a swashbuckling tone
